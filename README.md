# FaceRater photo and report guide

A practical checklist for taking a measurement photo, reading an illustrated face report, and comparing styling ideas without confusing a photo score with a verdict on appearance.

Maintained by the **FaceRater product team**. This is product documentation, not an independent review. It describes the public product as of 23 October 2026.

[Open FaceRater](https://face-rater.com/) · [Inspect the complete sample report](https://face-rater.com/report/sample) · [Read the measurement method](https://face-rater.com/methodology)

## Start with a photograph you can compare

The detector needs a clear view of the face. Before scanning:

- Face the camera with your eyes approximately level. Avoid a steep upward, downward or sideways angle.
- Use soft, even light. Strong side shadows make visual interpretation harder.
- Keep the forehead, eyes and jaw visible. Move hair away from the face outline.
- Use a relaxed expression for the measurement baseline. A smile changes the mouth and cheek geometry.
- Avoid very close wide-angle selfies. Keep a comfortable distance and use similar framing when comparing photos.

These steps improve comparability; they do not promise a particular score. The free scan maps **478 facial landmarks** and measures selected geometry in that photograph. Lens, pose, lighting and expression still matter.

## Know which result you are reading

| Result | Useful question | What to avoid assuming |
|---|---|---|
| Overall 0–100 score | How did the selected geometry combine in this photo? | That it predicts how everyone finds you attractive |
| Symmetry diagram | Which paired points align, and which differ? | That a single photo proves a permanent anatomical difference |
| Proportion diagram | Which measured spans produced this ratio? | That one reference ratio is a universal beauty ideal |
| Face outline estimate | Which shape and framing ideas might be worth comparing? | That an estimated label describes every angle or hairstyle |
| Two-photo comparison | What changed alongside lighting, pose or styling? | That a higher score proves a physical improvement |
| AI hairstyle concept | Do I prefer this length, volume or face framing? | That the edit preserves every facial detail or guarantees a salon result |

A useful reading starts with the diagram and the underlying spans. Then read the explanation and decide whether a practical suggestion fits your own preferences.

## Separate the free scan from the optional report

The first result is free: selected open readings, a photo-geometry score and a share card. Free image processing runs in the browser. Derived results may be retained for result-link and report functions; consult the product's data controls for the complete policy.

The optional **Face Report costs $17.49 once**, with no subscription. It includes:

- **26 readings across five illustrated facial-region chapters**;
- diagrams on the person's own photograph, explanations and practical styling suggestions;
- a personal plan and two-photo comparison;
- **six personal AI hairstyle concepts**;
- a manual color studio and a downloadable PDF.

The public sample is the quickest way to inspect the report format before purchasing. Product availability and the current offer are set by the live site.

## Read a chapter in four steps

1. **Locate the measurement.** Find the marked endpoints and supporting span on the portrait.
2. **Read the interpretation.** Ask what the ratio actually describes, rather than treating its value as a goal by itself.
3. **Choose a small styling experiment.** Compare hair framing, glasses, grooming or color ideas according to personal preference.
4. **Check the tradeoff.** Consider comfort, maintenance, budget and whether the suggestion suits the look you want.

You do not need to act on every reading. A report is more useful when it helps you shortlist a few preferences than when it becomes a checklist of things to change about yourself.

## Compare two photos deliberately

Use the [comparison worksheet](COMPARISON-WORKSHEET.md) to record what you changed before looking at the second score. Try to keep the camera, distance, expression and light similar. If several conditions change at once, record them; the difference cannot be attributed to one cause.

For hairstyle concepts, compare the edited image with the original at the same displayed size. Look at hair length and silhouette, then check whether the AI also changed facial details. Save a preferred concept as a discussion aid for a stylist, not as a promise of an identical result.

## What this guide and product do not establish

A landmark map does not capture voice, behavior, familiarity, culture or a person's worth. FaceRater's score is selected photo geometry, not a scientifically validated population attractiveness percentile, medical assessment or identity-recognition result. It is intended for adults exploring photographs and styling ideas.
